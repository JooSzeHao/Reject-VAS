# Leaflet review rubric

Score each criterion 1–5. Below 4 requires a written fix. A single 1 or 2 on any
criterion marked **(gate)** makes the leaflet not-ready-to-print regardless of total.

---

## 1. Readability & language

| # | Check | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Reading level ~ 6th–8th grade equivalent (see BM heuristics for Malay drafts) | /5 |
| 1.2 | No unexplained medical, pharmacy or administrative jargon **(gate)** | /5 |
| 1.3 | Every acronym expanded on first use, then used consistently | /5 |
| 1.4 | Sentences short, active, one idea each (target ≤ 15 words, hard cap ~20) | /5 |
| 1.5 | Second person ("anda"/"you"), not institutional third person | /5 |
| 1.6 | No mixed-language drift mid-sentence unless the term is the one patients actually use | /5 |

Read-aloud test: mark every sentence you stumble on, re-read, or can't finish in one
breath. Each one is a finding with its rewrite.

## 2. Patient-centric focus & messaging

| # | Check | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Answers "what's in it for me?" in the first 10 seconds of reading **(gate)** | /5 |
| 2.2 | Benefits lead; features and mechanics follow | /5 |
| 2.3 | Sign-up or access shown as numbered, countable steps | /5 |
| 2.4 | "How long does it take" and "what does it cost" answered explicitly | /5 |
| 2.5 | Eligibility stated plainly — who this is *not* for, too | /5 |
| 2.6 | "What happens next" after the patient acts | /5 |
| 2.7 | Top 3 real objections pre-answered (safety, delay, losing control, needing help) | /5 |

Objection inventory for a service leaflet — the draft should answer at least the first
three, in the patient's own framing:
- "Will my medicine actually arrive / be correct?"
- "What if I'm not home / don't have a smartphone?"
- "Do I lose my place, my doctor, or my appointment?"
- "Who do I call when something goes wrong?"

## 3. Visual structure & layout

| # | Check | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Skimmable in 15 seconds: headings alone tell the story | /5 |
| 3.2 | No paragraph over ~40 words; dense blocks converted to steps or lists | /5 |
| 3.3 | One idea per block, with visual separation | /5 |
| 3.4 | Key action in a callout, box or contrasting band | /5 |
| 3.5 | Icons/numbers carry meaning, not decoration; meaning survives without colour | /5 |
| 3.6 | Reading order obvious on a folded or double-sided piece (front → back cues) | /5 |
| 3.7 | Contact block findable without reading anything else | /5 |

Print realities to check when the draft is an HTML/PDF print file:
- Body text ≥ 11pt; ≥ 14pt for a large-print variant aimed at elderly readers **(gate)**
- Mono/photocopy safe: information never carried by colour alone
- Fits the page/fold without shrinking type below the floor — cut copy instead
- QR codes ≥ 25mm with a written alternative beside them (counter, phone number)
- Form fields long and tall enough to hand-write into (≥ 6mm line height)
- Nothing critical in the fold or trim zone

## 4. Tone & engagement

| # | Check | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Reassuring, modern, human — not bureaucratic, not salesy | /5 |
| 4.2 | Warm without being childish; respects an adult's intelligence | /5 |
| 4.3 | One primary CTA, unmistakable and physically prominent **(gate)** | /5 |
| 4.4 | CTA is a verb the patient can do today ("Beritahu staf hari ini") | /5 |
| 4.5 | Secondary CTA for the not-yet-ready reader ("ask for the full guide") | /5 |
| 4.6 | No fear, guilt, shaming or implied penalty **(gate)** | /5 |

## 5. Equity of access (often the highest-yield section)

| # | Check | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | A path for the patient with no smartphone and no internet **(gate)** | /5 |
| 5.2 | A path for the patient with no family to help | /5 |
| 5.3 | A path for low-literacy readers: icons, numbers, "show this to staff" | /5 |
| 5.4 | Language version(s) match the actual patient mix at the site | /5 |
| 5.5 | Nothing assumes a car, a fixed address, or daytime free hours | /5 |
| 5.6 | Caregivers and representatives explicitly allowed for and named | /5 |

## 6. Accuracy & governance

| # | Check | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Every number, hour, lead time and extension verified against a source **(gate)** | /5 |
| 6.2 | No promise stronger than the service guarantee | /5 |
| 6.3 | Data collected is named, with why it is needed | /5 |
| 6.4 | Refusal/opt-out is as plain and easy as opt-in, where one exists **(gate)** | /5 |
| 6.5 | Policy quotes are accurate, and framed as information not as threat | /5 |
| 6.6 | Version, date and owning department on the piece, for reprint control | /5 |

## 7. Decision-tree and form leaflets (apply when present)

- Every question is yes/no and answerable without clinical knowledge
- Every branch terminates in a named service and a first step
- The "none of these" branch exists and is not a dead end
- Outcomes carry the practical constraints right there (hours, lead time, pickup window)
- The form section states plainly who fills which part, and what happens to the paper
- A patient can complete the form with a pen, standing up, in under two minutes

---

## Scoring summary

- **34–40 per section-weighted average ≥ 4.3**, no gate below 4 → ready to print
- Any gate at 1–2 → Blocker, not ready
- Anything below 3 → Should fix at minimum

Report the total, but lead the review with the gates. A leaflet that scores 4.4 and
tells a patient to scan a QR code they cannot scan is a failed leaflet.
